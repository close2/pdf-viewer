# Q19 — Does the no-invented-marks fence move for part 1's transparency ban?

Source: RFC 0006 §10 question 5.
Status: **retired, not answered** — `A17` says part 1 is never a target, which removes the only
occasion this question had. No `A19` is owed.

## Why it needs the owner

Converting a transparent document to part 1 requires flattening, which replaces the producer's marks with a rendering of them, and that is exactly what the amended exclusion excludes.

## What the tree does meanwhile

Nothing built; the exclusion stands.

## Why it is retired rather than answered, and what survives it

`A17` closed part 1 outright — never a target — and flattening was the only thing that made this
question urgent, because parts 2 and 4 both permit transparency and neither asks a converter to
replace a producer's marks with a rendering of them.

**The general question underneath it is alive and is asked elsewhere**, which is why nothing is
lost by retiring this one. It is whether conformance ever licenses inventing marks, and it is put
concretely in `Q21` (may a converter write the annotation appearances this tree constructs?) and
in `Q48` (may an empty glyph stand in for `.notdef`?). Those are the cases that will actually
arise; this one now cannot.

## Recommendation

Do not move the fence. Parts 2, 3 and 4 permit transparency, so refusing part 1 by name and pointing at part 2 is the better answer.
