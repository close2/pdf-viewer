B39 already has a strong direct CPU-display-list witness: the ordinary alpha-.5 black stroke over white leaves the interior red channel at 127–129, while appending a contained degenerate closed subpath to the same Stroke command changes it to 64. The sample is wholly inside the ordinary stroke, so the red64 result does not rely on a normative claim that a zero-length subpath must itself be visible.

The normative oracle is effective §11.6.2, printed pp.415–416 / physical pp.430–431: one graphics object is one elementary transparency object, and portions of it shall not composite with one another even when its specification suggests overlap. Primary source wording is retained here; EC3 #103's mark concerns the separate opaque dash/subpath rule and is not generalized into a transparency exception. Parent's prior review has already made this distinction.
