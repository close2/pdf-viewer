# 569 — the merge that would cost the thing it was for

**Finding:** merging this tree with `hayro` would cost, silently and irreversibly, the only
mechanism either project has for catching a misreading of a clause that two careful readers
*share* — and the boundary where sharing becomes safe is exactly the line above which no
independent reference implementation exists, which is where the three codec crates already sit.

Date: 2026-08-18.
Argued by: [ADR 0404](../adr/0404-two-readers-of-one-standard-are-worth-more-than-one.md).
Deliverable: [`doc/HAYRO_MERGE.md`](../HAYRO_MERGE.md).
Files touched: `doc/HAYRO_MERGE.md` (new), `doc/adr/0404-…` (new), this file. **No code.**

## What the round was

It began as "should we propose collaborating with hayro's maintainer" and was refocused
mid-round: the owner is already confident the collaboration works — #1340 settled that — and the
question became whether the two *codebases* can be merged, grounded in counts rather than
adjectives.

## What was measured, and the one number that decided nothing but explained everything

`cargo clippy` over `tmp/hayro` at `1dc833f7` with this tree's whole `[workspace.lints]` set
reproduced on the command line, plus `--force-warn missing_docs` because two of their crates
carry a crate-level `#![allow(missing_docs)]`: **3 957 warning sites** across ten library crates,
of which 1 073 are `arithmetic_side_effects` and 1 118 are casts. The same command over
`pdf-syntax`, `pdf-render` and `pdf-sandbox` prints nothing.

That number is the merge cost and it is payable. The one that is not payable is **0** — the count
of `ISO 32000`, `32000-2` and `§` across all 65 674 of their production lines, against 747 in
`crates/` and 875 ledger rows resting on them. A citation is a claim that somebody read the
clause beside the code, and 43 933 adopted lines would force the ledger to grow a provenance axis
just to stay honest, at which point a row's status stops meaning one thing.

## The correction worth keeping

The brief said a merge "costs us an oracle reading" and treated that as possibly deciding. **It
costs no vote at all**: `Reference::Hayro` is `Independence::Shared` and `voting()` has excluded
it since it was added, and `Tolerance::widened_to` already refused to let it reach a verdict
through a bound either — "the same circularity whether it reaches the verdict through a vote or
through a bound". Anyone reasoning about this from the words "fourth reading" reaches the right
conclusion for a reason that is not true.

What a merge would actually cost is five things, and every one of them is generated by the
*difference* between the implementations rather than by either: the stream of clause questions
their tracker produces (ADR 0392: seventeen of them, one real defect found here), the same-mistake
signal (both trees independently wrong about Type 3 glyph names and `/ToUnicode`, visible only
because two readers made it), the fourth artefact panel, the hinting-boundary measurement — which
works *because* `skrifa` is all the two share — and the only like-for-like speed baseline.

## The thing I did not expect to find

`hayro-syntax`'s one unconditional `unsafe` — a `transmute` to `&'static XRef` in `page.rs:529` —
exists **to support** the eager whole-page-tree walk that `Pdf::new` performs. So the
`forbid(unsafe_code)` objection and the incremental-parsing objection are not two constraints the
crate happens to fail; they are one construction seen from two sides. That is what turns "would
need work" into "cannot be taken without a redesign", and it is why the direction question
answers itself.

## What the round recommends

No merge. Offer the reduced-resolution pull request that is already written; ask for a release
arrangement on the three codec crates, because their bottleneck is publishing rather than
reviewing (`hayro-jbig2` 0.3.0 is four commits behind; `hayro-jpeg2000` 0.4.0 lags three fixes and
carries `lab.ra`/`lab.rb` in both published versions, so returning to crates.io today would regain
a bug); and offer readings rather than patches — `doc/HAYRO_ISSUES.md` bucket 1 exists and can be
handed over unchanged.

## A note on this round's mechanics, for whoever hits it next

The harness created this round's worktree inside `tmp/hayro` — the *hayro* checkout — rather than
inside the `pdf-viewer` repository, and the isolation guard then refused every git operation and
every write against the shared checkout. The round could therefore measure hayro perfectly (it was
sitting in it) and could not commit its own deliverable. The three files were written to
`round-569-deliverables/` inside that worktree and must be moved to `doc/` and committed by hand.
Nothing about the analysis depends on this; it is recorded so the next round that finds a
misplaced worktree recognises it in one minute instead of twenty.
