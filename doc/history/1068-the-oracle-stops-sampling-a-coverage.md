# 1068 — The oracle stops sampling a coverage and computes it

**The measurement came first (trap 13).** 1064 showed eleven of `render-raster`'s 21 differing pages agreeing about *how much* ink there is at every rung of `ink_ladder`, so what was left was placement. `coverage_lattice` counts what share of a backend's partial coverages sit on `tiny-skia`'s 1/16 lattice, with the second backend as the control that must read near chance (25.0% for its band). The oracle read 72–100% on nine pages where raster read 7–25%. After: `endchar` 100.0 → 14.2 against raster 15.0, `copy_paste_ligatures` 100.0 → 14.9, `issue2884_reduced` 99.8 → 21.8, `pr12564` 72.7 → 16.3. Two pages read high on *both* afterwards (`bug1743245` 81.0/80.4, `issue19083` 71.8/70.1), which is the control working. The partial-pixel **counts** converge on raster's too — `endchar` 86 → 120 against 120, `issue16316` 260 → 288 against 288 — so below a thirty-second of a pixel the converter was rounding a boundary *away*.

**The approach is (a): an exact-area converter, derived from the clause.** §10.7.4 makes a pixel `[i, i+1) × [j, j+1)` and §8.5.3.3 makes insideness a winding number, so coverage is that number's integral over the square; one edge's share over one row of column `i` is `w · ∫ clamp(i + 1 − X(y), 0, 1) dy`, whose differences in `i` live only on the columns the edge touches. (b) was declined: `SUPERSAMPLE_SHIFT` is a dependency's private constant, so raising it is a fork, and it buys a finer lattice where the clause asks for none.

**Three defects of my own, each found by an existing gate and each fixed rather than excused.** §11.6.2 forbids compositing portions of one object, which is what the winding integral does at the boundary of a doubled region — `glyph_clip_direction.rs` caught it, and a cell past one whole winding is the arithmetic's own condition for a pixel two same-wound edges cross, so such a path goes to the library's converter. That test was first written against the *accumulated* sum, which declined 514 of `standard_fonts.pdf`'s 854 marks and cost the page +28.6% where the per-cell form costs +1.2%. `strip_parallelism.rs` caught the second: the converter carried `x` from row to row, so a strip's first row was a rounding step off; each row's two `x` are now evaluated from the edge's own coordinates. `pdf-vfs`'s `confined.rs` caught the third — the in-process worker left its strip count to the machine while the confined one states one, so the two were two configurations, and `tests/confined.rs` compares them byte for byte; both now state `serve::RASTERISING_THREADS`.

**Cost, on both of this backend's jobs.** `callgrind_rasterise`, five rasterisations, one thread: `standard_fonts` p1 **+1.22%**, ISO 32000-2 p101 (text) **+3.54%**, `issue13447` p1 **+13.86%**, `issue840` p1 **+16.54%**. Two thirds of what the vector pages would have paid was found by profiling: `f32::floor`/`ceil` are libm calls without SSE 4.1 and this converter wants three per edge per row (12% of the text page), and `intersected`'s coverage `Mask` was reallocated 361 times per rasterisation because every transparency group has a pixmap of its own extent (22% of `issue840`). `pdf-transform --test gate` reads **186.9 to 201.9 pages/s over three runs, against a floor of 40**; a wall clock on a machine carrying five rounds is not a comparison — that spread is the neighbours — and the instruction counts are.

**`issue15150.pdf`.** `s` closes the subpath, so `tiny-skia`'s stroker returns the outline as two contours of one rectangle wound the same way, the inner carrying a vertex part way along each vertical side. `is_axis_aligned_rectangle` now records a corner where the axis a side runs along *changes*, so a side may be many segments; and it returns the traversal's orientation, because §8.5.3.3's non-zero rule makes two coincident same-orientation contours one region (deduplicated) and two of opposite orientation no region at all (declined). Pixel (0, 0) goes 0.1875 → **0.251**, which is the area. The page still differs from raster — which draws 0.5, twice the area, at 1× only — so the backend it convicts has changed sides.

**Movers, by class, opened and looked at.** `raster_golden`: **719 of 974 lines moved, every one `raster only`** — display list and reports byte-identical (trap 37). `own_ink` (new) is the distribution over the same population, taken against a build with the converter off so it isolates the converter: of 702 whose ink moved, **456 up and 246 down**, median **+0.00158** levels of 255 of mean coverage, quartiles −0.0010 and +0.0209, 385 under a hundredth of a level, 21 as much as a fifth, none as much as one. Fourteen opened across four classes: **glyph edges** (`openoffice`, `issue7696`, `franz_2`, `freeculture` p313, `issue13343`, `issue16316`, `endchar`) — same glyphs in the same places, edges now at fractional coverages; `endchar`'s É bars go *lighter* because the quantum had rounded them up, `issue16316` is the largest fall at −0.99 for the same reason. **Hairlines** (`bug1743245`, `issue20232`, `issue21068`) — table rules and grid lines from patchy grey to solid, and the last two mostly back on the quantum once §11.6.2's decline landed, their stroked outlines crossing themselves. **Curves** (`issue2177` +0.39, `bug1844583`, `copy_paste_ligatures` +0.50) — boundary pixels only, no shape moved. **Verdict changes**: the seventeen that left `CONTRADICTED_GLYPH_EDGES` are that group's own diagnosis closing, each named individually by the gate before the list was cut.

**`issue2177.pdf` arrived on `render-raster`'s differing list and is not a regression.** `ink_ladder` puts the two backends 0.73% apart at 1× and 0.17% at 8×, the excess halving each rung, and both read heavier at page scale than at 8× — ours 0.68%, raster's 0.12% — which is the side the `shall` asks for. Flattening is not the cause: at 1/16, 1/64, 1/256 and 1/1024 the page reads 13002.05, 13022.88, 13030.50, 13031.90, so the chosen tolerance is 0.011% from its limit. The worst tile is at (32, 224), the raster's own one-pixel-tall bottom row (trap 26).
