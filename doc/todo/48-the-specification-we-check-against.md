# The copy of the standard this project checks itself against is lossy

Status: **proposed by the project owner on 2026-08-09** (`tmp/annotations-in-spec.txt`), and its
premise verified here before it was written down.
Priority: 48 — by convention, since the `40`–`49` band has in practice become *the project's own
instruments* (`43` a round's turnaround, `44` what `--trace` cannot see, `47` search). **Its real
weight is higher than the number**: principle 5 makes the specification the only source of truth,
and this item is about the fidelity of the copy that truth is read from. What keeps it out of the
defect band is that no *wrong* sentence has been found — only missing ones — and that step 1 below
is cheap enough to settle the question before anything is built.
Corpus: —, the subject is `doc/md/` and the fourteen documents under `doc/`
Code: a new tool beside `tools/conformance` and `tools/pdfref`; readers already in `pdf-model`

## The premise, checked rather than assumed

`doc/md/` is generated by a PDF-to-Markdown converter, and **annotations were ignored in the
conversion**. Counted directly out of the files:

| | `/Annots` | tagged |
|---|---|---|
| `ISO_32000-2_sponsored_EC3.pdf` | **882** | yes |
| `ISO-14289-2-2024-sponsored.pdf` | 88 | yes |
| `Well-Tagged-PDF-WTPDF-1.0.pdf` | 46 | yes |
| `ISO-14289-1-2014-sponsored.pdf` | 36 | yes |
| the other ten | 3 to 22 each | yes |

**All fourteen state a `/StructTreeRoot`**, which settles the hedge the proposal was careful to
leave open: these are tagged documents, so §14.7's logical structure — and `Tree::logical_range`,
which this tree has had since session 296 — gives reading order, headings and table structure
outright. A layout converter's inference is not doing work here that we would have to replace.

## Why this is not speculative: the conversion has been caught three times

Each of these is in an ADR or a round's record, and each was found by accident rather than by
looking:

- **Session 401** — the conversion drops two of `/IT`'s three values and its default line, visible
  in `pdftotext` over the same PDF.
- **Session 413** — it breaks words across lines (`hierarch y`, `text-tospeech`), which
  `quote::normalise` does not repair, so **the conformance gate rejected two correct quotations**
  until they were shortened. The instrument was wrong about the standard.
- **Session 414** — Table 354 does state `/PronunciationLexicon` and `/AF`, but only past a 9 000-byte
  window, because the conversion splits it across two header rows. A shorter read would have
  produced two false positives.

Annotations are a fourth loss and potentially the worst: if the sponsored EC3 documents record
errata or committee notes as annotations, a quotation of body text can quote a sentence an erratum
has corrected — precisely the failure principle 5 exists to prevent.

## The hazard, and it is a principle-5 hazard

**The conformance gate must not check quotations against a copy of the standard that this project
generated.** It verifies 576 rustdoc blockquotes and 6032 citations against `doc/md/`; if we produce
that file, a defect in our extractor becomes a defect in the standard we check ourselves against,
and a wrong quotation verifies against our own error. That inserts this project between the
specification and the check, which is the one direction the rule forbids.

The way to keep it honest is an **independent second opinion**: `pdftotext` already plays that role
in `tests/text_extraction.rs`, and requiring agreement between our extraction and a foreign one for
any span a quotation lands in preserves the check's independence.

## The staged plan, and the order is the point

1. **Census first** — this project's own rule, and what made §10.5 a small change rather than a brave
   one. How many annotations, of which subtypes, carrying what; whether any of them is an erratum or
   a committee note rather than a bookmark or a link; and what the fourteen documents' structure
   trees actually contain. **The census may end the item**: 882 link annotations are worth nothing
   here, and 882 review comments would change how every clause in the tree is read.
2. **A sidecar, not a replacement.** A tool in `tools/` that emits what the conversion *dropped* —
   annotations keyed to clause and page — leaving `doc/md/` and all 6608 verified references
   untouched. Zero migration cost, immediate benefit, and it is the step that makes the information
   usable without betting anything on it.
3. **A disagreement sweep**, in the established style: compare our extraction against `doc/md/` and
   report every span where they differ. That finds the remaining losses mechanically instead of one
   accident at a time, and it is the same shape as `doc/todo/01`'s eleven sweeps.
4. **Only then** decide whether to switch the substrate — and the migration is the real constraint:
   a new conversion changes every line number and every quotation's whitespace, so switching
   wholesale means re-verifying 6032 citations and 576 quotations. Session 413 looked at a 417-span
   migration and declined it.

## Two practical notes, both from the proposal and both right

- **No new API is needed.** `pdf-model` is the API and the consumer is an in-tree Rust tool;
  `viewer-ffi`'s 43 entry points are for embedding, not for this. And **the gate must not parse a
  1023-page PDF on every run** — session 385 spent a whole round taking a round from 608 s to 268 s.
- **Whatever is generated is still the specification.** ADR 0187 put those documents behind an
  encrypted zip because the owner is not licensed to redistribute them; a derived Markdown carries
  the same restriction and stays under the same `.gitignore` and zip discipline.

## What would make this item wrong

If the census finds the annotations are links and bookmarks, steps 2 to 4 buy nothing and the item
closes with the count written down. Say so plainly in that case: an item that survives its own
census unchanged is one nobody re-checked.
