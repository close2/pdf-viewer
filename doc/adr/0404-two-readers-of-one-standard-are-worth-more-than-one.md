# 0404. Two readers of one standard are worth more than one

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: accepted
Round: 569

## Context

The project owner asked whether this tree and `LaurenzV/hayro` — the other feature-complete
pure-Rust PDF implementation, three of whose crates we already ship — could become one codebase,
and what that would mean.

The question was asked after the collaboration had already proved itself. A reconstruction-midpoint
fix for `hayro-jpeg2000` was offered on 2026-08-05 and merged as #1340 on 2026-08-16, in the
maintainer's own reimplementation, with co-authorship preserved; `doc/HAYRO_PR_REDUCED_RESOLUTION.md`
holds a second fix that is written, rebased and measured but not yet offered. So this ADR is not
about whether to work with them. It is about whether to stop being two.

`doc/HAYRO_MERGE.md` is the evaluation. This records the decision and the two arguments that
decide it, because those are the parts that will still matter when the counts have decayed.

## Decision

**No merge of the syntax, interpreter, font or rendering layers, in either direction.** Keep and
deepen the extracted lower half that already exists — the three codec crates — and add two things
that cost no code: a release arrangement for those crates, and an exchange of specification
*readings* rather than patches.

Three moves follow, in order:

1. Offer the reduced-resolution pull request, which is ready.
2. Ask for release cadence, or co-maintainer rights, on `hayro-jbig2`, `hayro-jpeg2000` and
   `hayro-ccitt`.
3. Offer `doc/HAYRO_ISSUES.md` bucket 1 — seventeen of their issues answered against ISO 32000-2
   with the clause quoted — and the JPEG 2000 conformance rig that measured #1340.

## The two arguments that decide it

Everything else in `doc/HAYRO_MERGE.md` is cost, and cost can be paid. These two cannot.

### 1. The boundary is where an independent reference implementation stops existing

A JBIG2 or JPEG 2000 decoder is checkable without a second PDF reader: ISO/IEC 15444-5 publishes
reference software, and `pdf-model/tests/jpeg2000.rs` already decodes all 30 corpus codestreams
through `hayro-jpeg2000` *and* through `opj_decompress` and compares them exactly, holding the 13
differences by name. Shared code there is judged against a truth neither project wrote, so
sharing it carries no principle-5 risk at all.

**The content-stream interpreter has no such reference.** Above that line a second implementation
*is* the instrument; below it, the reference software is the instrument and a second
implementation is only a second cost. That asymmetry is not a heuristic about where collaboration
is comfortable — it is a statement about what can be measured, and it draws the boundary by
itself. The three codec crates sit exactly on it, which is why the extraction that a merge would
have to perform has in effect already been performed.

### 2. A merge costs no oracle *vote*, and that is the wrong thing to count

The obvious objection — that merging hayro's interpreter into ours would silently turn a fourth
independent reading into a mirror — is half right and half already answered.

It is already answered because **hayro does not vote**. `Reference::Hayro`'s `independence()` is
`Independence::Shared("shares skrifa, flate2, zune-jpeg, hayro-jbig2 and hayro-jpeg2000 with us")`,
`voting()` filters to `Independent`, and `Tolerance::widened_to` refused to let hayro reach a
verdict through a bound either, on the ground that it is "the same circularity whether it reaches
the verdict through a vote or through a bound". No verdict in this tree would move if hayro
vanished.

It is half right because the losses are real and none of them is a vote:

- **the stream of clause questions** — ADR 0392 found seventeen of their issues to be questions
  about a clause, one of which found a real defect here (`/Rows` is not a row count);
- **the same-mistake signal** — both trees independently concluded a Type 3 glyph name says
  nothing about the character, and both were wrong; that is visible only while there are two
  readers, because what makes it visible is that the mistake was made *twice*;
- the fourth artefact panel, the hinting-boundary measurement (`ours + hayro` closest on 651 of
  786 ambiguous pages, which works *because* we share `skrifa` and nothing else), and the only
  like-for-like speed baseline that exists.

**Every one of those is generated by the difference between the two implementations, not by
either of them.** The first two are the ones worth the ADR: they are how a misreading of a clause
gets caught at all, and this project has no other mechanism that catches the misreading two
careful readers *share*. Merging is the one action that cannot be undone by a later round, and it
is the one that removes them.

## What was counted

The full table is `doc/HAYRO_MERGE.md` §3. Three figures carry the argument.

- **3 957 lint sites** across hayro's ten library crates under this tree's `[workspace.lints]` set
  — 1 073 `arithmetic_side_effects`, 1 118 casts, 154 missing docs, 107 `unwrap`/`expect` — against
  **0** for `pdf-syntax`, `pdf-render` and `pdf-sandbox` under the same command.
- **0 clause citations** in 65 674 production lines, against 747 in `crates/` and 875 ledger rows
  resting on them. The ledger would need a provenance axis to absorb uncited code, and a row's
  status would stop meaning one thing.
- **43 933 lines of overlap** — 67% of their production code; 29% is already ours as a dependency
  and 4% is out of scope. Meanwhile 68 744 lines, 37% of *our* tree, have no counterpart at all.

And one structural fact that rules the parser out on its own: `hayro-syntax` carries an
unconditional `unsafe { core::mem::transmute(…) }` to `&'static XRef`, and it is there *to
support* the eager whole-page-tree walk in `Pdf::new`. `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` on every crate
touching PDF bytes and "a 500-page document must open no slower than a 5-page one" are two
`CLAUDE.md` constraints, and that one construction violates both — so they are not two objections
but one, and it cannot be taken without a redesign.

## Consequences

- `doc/HAYRO_MERGE.md` is written for the owner to read before contacting the maintainer, and it
  carries the draft message.
- The un-pin condition for `hayro-jpeg2000` is unchanged and still has four parts; move 1 addresses
  the fourth and move 2 addresses why three completed parts are still not on crates.io.
- **Nothing in this tree changes.** This round produced an argument, and it should be reopened
  rather than obeyed if move 2 is declined — in which case `doc/HAYRO_MERGE.md` §8's last item
  names vendoring, not merging, as the next position to price.

## Alternatives rejected

- **We absorb hayro.** Licence-clean (`Apache-2.0 OR MIT`, and the MIT arm is free to take), but
  it is a rewrite of somebody else's code that buys nothing we lack, and it pays the whole cost
  above for it.
- **hayro absorbs us.** Requires dual-licensing our MIT tree, which is the owner's decision alone,
  and hands direction to a project whose maintainer has stated "my main priority is definitely
  speed as opposed to 100% correctness". Two projects may hold different priorities and still help
  each other every week; one codebase cannot hold both.
- **A shared font stack above `skrifa`.** Rejected on the measurement: the ours-to-hayro closeness
  over the ambiguous bucket is the only reading this project has of the font half of trap 9, and
  it exists only because `skrifa` is *all* the two share.
- **Waiting.** Rejected because move 1 is a pull request that is already written, and holding it
  costs hayro's users a fix for no reason.
