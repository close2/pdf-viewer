# 1163 — A page the structure tree does not reach is an artifact, so the appended page is described

Status: accepted and **built**. Session 1163.
Context: `crates/pdf-transform/src/archive/tagged.rs` (new),
`crates/pdf-transform/src/archive/{preserve,prepare,rewrite,mod}.rs`,
`crates/pdf-transform/tests/archive.rs`.
Amends: `doc/adr/1025` §5's third row and §6's first bullet, which refused every tagged document
because "[t]he structure entries an appended page owes … are not built". They are built here.
Builds: `doc/adr/1014` (the permission and the bill), `doc/adr/0954` (what an appending remedy
owes), `doc/adr/1099` and `doc/adr/1123` (the marks), `doc/questions/A58` (the owner's ratification).
Clauses: ISO 32000-2 §7.9.7, §14.6.2, §14.7.2 (Tables 354, 355), §14.7.5.2 (Table 357),
§14.7.5.4 (Table 359), §14.8.2.2 (§14.8.2.2.1), §14.8.4.4 (Table 365), §14.8.4.5 (Table 366),
§14.8.6.1, Annex L; ISO 19005-2 sections 6.7.1, 6.7.2.1, 6.7.2.2, 6.7.3.3.

## 1. The owner's note, claim by claim

`doc/adr_revisit/1025-a-page-of-the-packet-the-producer-wrote.md` is an argument to test, not an
instruction. Three claims, and the third is wrong in a way that does not touch the conclusion.

- **"The permission is not in doubt."** *Held.* ADR 1014 §3's last paragraph settles it in as many
  words — the entries "are what makes the first act finish as a conforming file", the ruling is
  one and not two — and §5 bills them by name. `CLAUDE.md`'s third amendment carries the same
  sentence, ratified by `A58`.
- **"A real document loses the remedy."** *Held, and measured again here.* §7 below.
- **"The machinery now exists in `pdf-fuse` (ADRs 0830, 0834, 1151)."** *Does not hold as stated.*
  `pdf-fuse` is RFC 0003's FUSE face and has no structure code at all; ADRs 0830 and 0834 are
  `pdf-transform`'s `structure.rs`, and ADR 1151 is `pdf-model`'s reader. Nor is that machinery
  reusable here even under its right name: `structure.rs`'s `Carry` plans a tree for a document
  **derived** from others, renumbering keys as pages cross into a new object table, and this
  conversion edits the document it was given. What ADRs 0830, 0834 and 1151 actually supply is the
  *reading* — which is worth more, because it is where the clause was already argued and where the
  shapes a producer really writes were already counted.

## 2. Why the page is described at all, and why it is not an Artifact

§14.8.2.2.1 divides a document's content into two classes, and its last sentence closes the
division: content no structure tree reaches *is* an artifact, whether or not anything marked it as
one. So there is no third option. Leaving the appended page out of the tree does not leave it
undescribed; it *classifies* it, as one of "artifacts, whether generated by the PDF writer in the
course of pagination, layout, or other mechanical processes or introduced by the document author
for decoration or other purposes that are not relevant for understanding the content of the
document". The preserved packet is none of those — it is the producer's own metadata, kept as
content precisely because a reader of the archive should still be able to read it. Marking it an
artifact would hide it from the text-to-speech engine §14.8.2.2.1 spends its own NOTE on, and
would be this conversion asserting something false about content it was asked to preserve.

That is why the refusal ADR 1025 §5 wrote was right at the time and is not a refusal that could
be dropped: the answer is not *stop checking*, it is *build the entries*.

## 3. The hierarchy, and the two tables that fix it

One `Part` per preserved object, holding one `P` per line the producer wrote.

- Table 365 makes `Part` a grouping element that "[e]ncloses a grouping of structure elements
  without consideration for their hierarchy". A packet's lines have no hierarchy; `Sect`, the
  neighbouring type, is for "a grouping of structure elements with consideration for their
  hierarchy", which would be a claim about the content.
- Table 366 makes `P` "[a] low-level division of content" and adds that "it may enclose any
  low-level division of content". A line is one.
- **No `H` and no `Hn`.** A heading is a claim this conversion cannot make: the producer wrote no
  heading, and ISO 19005-2 section 6.7.1 advises a writer against adding structural information
  the source does not state. The same sentence is why nothing gets a `/T` title, an `/Alt` or an
  `/ActualText` — every one of those is words, and ADR 1014's fence is that nothing goes on the
  page, or into what describes it, whose content did not come from the file.

**A wrapped line is one `P`, not two.** ADR 1025 §4's fifth choice breaks a long line at the
measure and adds nothing to mark the break, so the division of content is the producer's line and
not the piece of it that fitted. Table 357's marked-content reference is what makes that
expressible: it carries a `/Pg` of its own, which the table says takes precedence over any the
containing structure element states, so one `P` can hold two sequences on two pages. Every
child is written in that form rather than as a bare integer, so there is one code path and no
element depends on inheriting a page.

**The grouping element hangs from the structure tree root.** Table 354 admits it outright: `/K` is
"[t]he immediate child or children of the structure tree root in the structure hierarchy. The
value may be either a dictionary representing a single structure element or an array of such
dictionaries." It is also the smallest edit available — one entry of one dictionary the producer
wrote — and it keeps the amendment's other promise, that the producer's own elements are not
rewritten. The alternative, adding the group under the producer's own top-level element, would
claim the appended page is part of the logical document the producer wrote, which it is not.

### Annex L, and why it is not the rule these elements answer to

Annex L's Table L.2 gives `StructTreeRoot` exactly one child and makes it a `Document`, which a
second top-level child would break. Two reasons it does not bind here, and the first is the one
that settles it:

- Annex L governs "the standard structure namespace for PDF 2.0", and §14.8.6.1 says which
  namespace an element with no `/NS` entry is in: "the **default** standard structure namespace
  shall be `http://iso.org/pdf/ssn`", which that clause has just defined as the **PDF 1.7**
  namespace. These elements state no `/NS`. Their nesting rule is ISO 32000-1:2008 clause 14.8's,
  which prints no such table — and that edition is exactly what ISO 19005-2 section 6.7.2.1
  requires a Level A file to meet.
- A real corpus document already settles what the producers do: `issue8702.pdf`'s root states an
  array of two `Slide` elements and no `Document` at all. A rule this conversion could only keep
  by rewriting the producer's tree is not a rule an appended page can be held to.

## 4. §14.7.5.4, written forwards

Three entries, and each is the clause's own:

- **A marked-content sequence per line.** §14.7.5.2: "[t]he operators shall be bracketed as a
  marked-content sequence between BDC and EMC operators" with "a property list … containing an
  MCID entry". The tag is `P`, because the clause asks for it — "it should be the same as the
  structure type of the associated structure element" — and the property list is inline, which
  §14.6.2 permits because its one value is a direct object.
- **`/StructParents` on every appended page**, which Table 359 makes "[r]equired for all content
  streams containing marked-content sequences that are structural content items".
- **One `/Nums` entry per appended page**, whose value is the array of parent elements indexed by
  identifier, and `/ParentTreeNextKey` moved past every key used. The producer's own
  `ParentTreeNextKey` is honoured where it satisfies the clause — "an integer value greater than
  any that is currently in use as a key" — and corrected where it does not, because a key already
  in use would put this page's array where one of the producer's pages' belongs.

The identifiers begin again at zero on each page, which §14.7.5.2 makes correct — an identifier
"uniquely identifies the marked-content sequence within its content stream" — and which its NOTE
asks for: the values "need to be as small as possible to conserve space in the array".

**The content stream became one text object per line**, on a tagged page and an untagged one
alike. A sequence has to bracket exactly one line's operators, and §9.4.2's `Td` is relative
inside a text object and absolute after a `BT`, so each line states its own baseline. Writing the
untagged page the same way keeps one layout rather than two, at the cost of a `BT`/`ET` pair per
line in a stream that is flate-encoded anyway.

## 5. The refusals that stay, each a shape rather than a shortfall

`doc/rfc/0007`'s rule is that a refusal which cannot become an "ask" is the thing to avoid. Four
stay, and each names a shape an operator or a later round can answer:

| shape | why |
|---|---|
| **`/MarkInfo /Marked true` with no `/StructTreeRoot`** | the claim is the document's; with no tree, the page could only be described by this conversion writing one, which ISO 19005-2 section 6.7.1 advises against |
| **the catalog states `/StructTreeRoot` as a direct dictionary** | Table 355 makes a structure element's `/P` "[r]equired; shall be an indirect reference", and there is no reference to a direct object |
| **a `/ParentTree` with `/Kids`, or a `/Nums` stated by reference** | §7.9.7's number tree may spread its keys, and putting one into the right leaf with every `/Limits` above it moved is a rewrite of the producer's tree; the same shape `LABELS_NOT_EXTENDABLE` already refuses |
| **marks, in a tagged document** | §6 |

## 6. The one shape this does not build: an appearance stream's structure type

A forbidden annotation's marks — whether ADR 1123 relocates them onto the producer's own page or
ADR 1099 gives them a page of their own — are the producer's real content, so §14.8.2.2.1 puts
them in the structure tree too. Table 355 makes `/S` required, and **what an annotation's artwork
is semantically is a fact only its producer held**. `Annot` is not the answer: Table 368 makes that
type "[e]ither an association between the content enclosed by the Annot structure element and one
or more corresponding PDF annotations, or a mechanism to include one or more PDF annotations in
the structure tree", and the annotation is exactly what this remedy has had to remove. `Figure`
would be a claim, and ISO 19005-2 section 6.7.5 would then want an `/Alt` — words this program
does not have.

So a tagged document asking for the marks is refused with that sentence, and the refusal is
shaped to become an ask: an operator who knows what the artwork is can say so, which is
`doc/rfc/0007` section 0.2's `supply` at a site that does not have one yet.

## 7. What it does, measured

`doc/pdf.js/test/pdfs`, 974 documents, PDF/A-2b, `doc/profiles/only-metadata-loss.toml`: nine fail
`metadata/properties-use-known-schemas`. **Before: 7 preserved on a page, 2 refused. After: 9
preserved, 0 refused** — both counts printed by one script over the same corpus, the first from a
build of `HEAD`. `issue8702.pdf` was the structure-tree refusal and `issue16067.pdf` §8's;
`issue8702.pdf`'s preserved packet now lands on a page its own tree reaches, under a `Part` beside
the two `Slide` elements its producer wrote. Neither file is *written* — both fail other
requirements this converter still owes, `fonts/font-programs-embedded` among them — which is the
same answer they gave before and is a fact about those documents rather than about this remedy.

The fixture in `tests/archive.rs` is the end-to-end proof and it is proved on the copy: the output
is re-opened, `pdf_model::structure::Tree` — the reader `viewer-core`'s accessibility tree uses —
walks the new elements, `ParentTree::for_page` resolves the appended page's key, and
`Tree::logical_text` reads the producer's own packet back off the page in §14.8.2.5.1's logical
content order.

The fixture is also converted at **Level A**, which is the level ISO 19005-2 section 6.7 binds at
all, and the output conforms: the validator that checked the 2b conversion then holds the same file
to the logical-structure rules as well, so what the page owes is checked rather than merely
written.

## 8. The second refusal, which fell out on the way

The corpus's other refused document — `issue16067.pdf` — was refused for "a character
`/WinAnsiEncoding` has no code for", and its packet states no character outside ASCII at all. Its
line terminator is CRLF, and `is_set` made the face search demand a glyph for a carriage return
that `wrapped` had already trimmed off the end of every line: a refusal over a character no page
was going to carry.

§7.2.3 is the reading. "The CARRIAGE RETURN (0Dh) and LINE FEED (0Ah) characters, also called
newline characters, shall be treated as end-of-line (EOL) markers. The combination of a CARRIAGE
RETURN followed immediately by a LINE FEED shall be treated as one EOL marker." So both are the
break rather than something set, which is the argument `is_set` already made about the line feed;
and `wrapped` now reduces the clause's three spellings to one before it splits, so a packet
terminated with lone carriage returns states the breaks its producer wrote rather than becoming one
line. That is why the measured result below is 9 of 9 rather than 8.
